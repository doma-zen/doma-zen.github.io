The internal memory

A memory engine that grows about your business

One of the biggest frustrations with AI is that it forgets. You explain your business, it helps once, and next time you start again from zero. This is the opposite of that. Doma builds an internal memory of your business, your processes, your documents, your questions, your prices, and what actually happened, and that memory grows every time you use it. That growing memory is the MantraVault at work: your internal mantra, held in separate public and private business files that answer questions from what your business has actually put in.

Memory like that is not free, and we say so plainly. You see what the service uses and what it costs before you approve it. The technical detail on how that use is counted sits in the Technical details section below, behind a click.

It grows, on purpose

Every document you add, every question answered, every process you correct, and every interaction that finishes makes the memory larger and sharper. You are not renting a stranger’s memory. You are growing your own.

More than one index, owned by you

A business rarely needs just one set of files. It can hold several sets at once, for different jobs and different audiences, and each one is owned by that business. We manage them, improve them, and keep them separate, the way you would keep separate files for separate work, only these answer when you ask.

You decide what faces the public

That split is also the security. You decide what is public facing and what stays private, and the design separates the two so that line holds. We then optimize hardest around the public facing area, because that is where customers meet it, while private memory stays private.

Public facing index

What customers may see and ask about: what you do, what it costs, how to book, what you promise. Kept tight, current, and fast, because this is the face people meet.

Private internal index

Processes, policies, staff notes, and the who, how, when, what, and what’s next inside the business. For you and the people you name, never for customers.

Other owned indexes

A trade, a location, a product line, a project: its own index when the work is different enough to deserve one, still owned by the same business, still managed the same way.

Other tools answer from what your business already wrote down. Most of what your business knows was never written down. Doma Mantra learns that part, by asking, and keeps it in a knowledge base your business owns.

Technical details +

The original wording, kept for readers who want the machinery: memory like that costs tokens, and it costs energy, every time it scans, indexes, and answers. So it has to be metered smartly, split into the right indexes, and routed well, or a business pays to remember things nobody should even be able to see.

Metered honestly: tokens, energy, and the price at the time

Every interaction uses tokens. Running the models and the indexes uses electricity. And electricity does not cost the same all day: the price changes with the time, so the same interaction can cost more at one hour than at another. Honest metering has to know all three: how many tokens an interaction used, how much energy it used, and what electricity cost at that time.

That is why the measure we work to improve is kilowatt-hours per interaction. As an internal mantra system gets better indexed, better routed, and less wasteful, each answer should take less energy to produce. Improvement is not just a smarter answer. It is a cheaper, lighter answer, too.

An ACD router for your documents and your knowledge base

In a call center, an ACD (automatic call distribution) router does not answer the call itself. It reads what the call is about, how urgent it is, and who has the skill for it, then routes it to the right place. Doma does that same job for knowledge.

A question comes in, on any medium. The router reads it and sends it to the right index, the right document, and the right knowledge base, public or private, depending on who is asking and what they are allowed to see. Nothing has to hunt through everything. The right source gets the question first.

The index, in plain words

Read it like a ladder, not a league table. There is no score to buy and no number handed out here. A business can see which step it is standing on, and what the next step up asks of it.

  1. STEP 1 · STARTING POINT

    Answers live in people’s heads, in inboxes, and in five different places. Customers repeat themselves, and every answer depends on who happens to pick up.

  2. STEP 2 · ORGANIZED

    The documents, prices, promises, and policies are in one place, under control, changed once and seen by everyone who should see them.

  3. STEP 3 · CONNECTED

    Every way in, web, text, chat, email, and phone, routes to that same knowledge, with public and private kept apart and the right source answering first.

  4. STEP 4 · IMPROVING

    Every interaction is reflected on and pushed back out as an improvement. The pulse gets faster, the business gets fitter, and each answer costs less to produce than the last.

  5. STEP 5 · PROVEN

    The business knows itself, remembers everything that matters, and says what is true in plain words. Trust stops being a claim and becomes a record the business can review.

Maturity, said simply, is the distance between step 1 and step 5: from answers that depend on memory and mood, to answers that come from knowledge and experience, every time, on every channel.

Two indicators sit at the heart of the index. The first is maturity itself: which step the business is on. The second is the trust relationship, the Trust Index idea in plain form: do customers believe this business will do what it said, because its record shows that it does? A business can look busy and still be immature. It is mature when its answers are true and its promises are kept, and both can be shown.

Investors +

Doma serves the middle tier on purpose: not the cheapest, not a rip-off. A better customer experience at a fairer price, doing good and making money as the same motion.

The product companies pay for is the brain: a system that learns a business by asking the right questions. That is the knowledge race. The Interaction Center is the execution: scheduling the interaction and building the relationship across website, web chat, text, email, and phone, not telephone alone. The telephone is only a medium, a route to an answer. Leases, occupancy, repeat business, and trust are how it gets measured.

Underneath the brain is an internal memory engine, MantraVault, that grows about each business and is held in more than one owned retrieval index, public facing and private kept apart, with questions routed to the right document and knowledge base the way an ACD routes a call. That memory is metered in tokens and energy per interaction, with the price of electricity at the time in view, and the measure to improve is kilowatt-hours per interaction. No flat cost is claimed here, because that price moves.

Read the whole thing as a business maturity index, not just a website, with two indicators watched above the rest: how mature the business has become, and how strong the trust relationship is. A business climbs from scattered, to recorded, to connected, to learning, to wise.

The knowledge built for a client stays an asset, including a Rent-to-Own Option to own that store of knowledge outright. Affiliates bring their industries; Doma is the platform underneath.

Privacy +

What a business tells its Doma system is used to make that business healthier. It is never sold, never used against them, and never shared outside the people they name.

Documents are shared by name, group, or role only, with version history, so a business always knows who saw what, and when it changed.
