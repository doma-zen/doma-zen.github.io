Phone Ping Synchronization

HOW IT WORKS

The whisper above is for a call happening now. This is for the call that keeps missing. Nobody cold-calls and nobody plays phone tag: a ping proposes a time, the two schedules synchronize, and at the agreed moment the system makes the call, with the question carried into the room. One flow, six steps:

  1. 1

    The ping proposes. Either side can start it. The message reads: “I’m calling you, let’s conference when we can,” with the topic and a proposed time.

  2. 2

    Confirm, counter, or decline. The other side confirms the time, offers a better one, or declines. Nothing counts as agreed until both sides stand on the same time, and a decline is recorded and respected, never retried in secret.

  3. 3

    The reminder is acknowledged. A confirmed time sends a reminder, and the reminder is acknowledged. Nobody assumes anybody saw anything.

  4. 4

    The readiness check. At the moment itself, both sides are asked: ready to go, reschedule, or cancel. A cancel closes it as cancelled, a decision on the record, never confused with a call that simply went unanswered.

  5. 5

    The bridge. On ready from both sides, the person answering is called first and hears the whisper: who is calling, and the question, in the caller’s own words. Then the caller joins. “Connected.”

  6. 6

    It is written down. Completed, missed, or cancelled, the outcome lands in the record, where it is counted on the weekly and monthly reports.

Where it stands today, stated plainly: the call half is proven, as a private demonstration on our own phones. The texting half activates when carrier approval completes; until then, no text is sent.

Technical details +

The original wording, kept for readers who want the machinery: memory like that costs tokens, and it costs energy, every time it scans, indexes, and answers. So it has to be metered smartly, split into the right indexes, and routed well, or a business pays to remember things nobody should even be able to see.

Metered honestly: tokens and energy

Every interaction uses tokens. Running the models and the indexes uses electricity. And the energy an interaction draws is not the same all day: demand changes with the time, so the same interaction can draw more at one hour than at another. Honest metering has to know all three: how many tokens an interaction used, how much energy it used, and what electricity cost at that time.

That is why the measure we work to improve is kilowatt-hours per interaction. As an internal mantra system gets better indexed, better routed, and less wasteful, each answer should take less energy to produce. Improvement is not just a smarter answer. It is a cheaper, lighter answer, too.

An ACD router for your documents and your knowledge base

In a call center, an ACD (automatic call distribution) router does not answer the call itself. It reads what the call is about, how urgent it is, and who has the skill for it, then routes it to the right place. Mantra does that same job for knowledge.

A question comes in, on any medium. The router reads it and sends it to the right index, the right document, and the right knowledge base, public or private, depending on who is asking and what they are allowed to see. Nothing has to hunt through everything. The right source gets the question first.

The index, in plain words

Read it like a ladder, not a league table. There is no score to buy and no number handed out here. A business can see which step it is standing on, and what the next step up asks of it.

  1. STARTING POINT

    Answers live in people’s heads, in inboxes, and in five different places. Customers repeat themselves, and every answer depends on who happens to pick up.

  2. ORGANIZED

    The documents, prices, promises, and policies are in one place, under control, changed once and seen by everyone who should see them.

  3. CONNECTED

    Every way in, web, text, chat, email, and phone, routes to that same knowledge, with public and private kept apart and the right source answering first.

  4. IMPROVING

    Every interaction is reflected on and pushed back out as an improvement. The pulse gets faster, the business gets fitter, and each answer costs less to produce than the last.

  5. PROVEN

    The business knows itself, remembers everything that matters, and says what is true in plain words. Trust stops being a claim and becomes a record the business can review.

Maturity, said simply, is the distance between step 1 and step 5: from answers that depend on memory and mood, to answers that come from knowledge and experience, every time, on every channel.

Two indicators sit at the heart of the index. The first is maturity itself: which step the business is on. The second is the trust relationship, the Trust Index idea in plain form: do customers believe this business will do what it said, because its record shows that it does? A business can look busy and still be immature. It is mature when its answers are true and its promises are kept, and both can be shown.

The refinement loop: how it keeps getting righter

Going live is the start, not the finish. Every real interaction is read, and what it teaches goes back into the base. Five moves, in a loop:

  1. 1

    It reads. Every question and answer is read against what the base knows: what was asked, what was answered, and how sure the answer was.

  2. 2

    It misses honestly. What it does not know, it says it does not know, to the customer and on the record. The miss goes on the miss list. It is never papered over with a guess.

  3. 3

    The owner teaches once. The miss becomes a question to the one person who knows. They answer it once, in their own words, and never have to answer it again.

  4. 4

    We verify, in the open. The question is asked again and the fixed answer is shown working, not asserted. The correction sits at the top of the record; the original stays on file.

  5. 5

    It compounds. The base is righter next week than this week, and the monthly report counts what was learned, so the improvement is a number, not a feeling.
