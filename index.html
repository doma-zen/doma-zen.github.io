🫧 The Mantra Brain, bubble mind map, how we learn

This is how the brain works, in plain words. We take what a business teaches us, rip it apart into pieces, and put those pieces on a mind map, as bubbles. Then we keep learning, round after round.

Mantra
heart Schedules People Promises made Promises kept Lessons learned

How to read the bubbles: the center bubble is the mantra, the heart. The bubbles around it are the topics we learned. Bigger bubble = stronger, more confident, more proof behind it. Smaller bubble = weaker, still learning, needs more questions. Lines between bubbles are connections, "this promise depends on that schedule," "this person owns that job."

  1. Rip it apart, website, documents, interview, all broken into pieces, with source and version kept on every piece.
  2. Mind map it, pieces become bubbles, bubbles connect, the map shows what the business really is, at a glance.
  3. Continuous learn, every interaction, every correction, every kept or broken promise moves a bubble: up, down, bigger, smaller, stronger, weaker. The map never freezes.

That is the secret sauce, in a picture: not a list, not a pile of documents, a living map, that learns. Ask once, remember everything, show it back, and watch the bubbles move.

📥 Our ingestion philosophy, how we learn a business, without forcing one on it

Ingestion is simple, and it is not a trick: take what a business already says about itself, and put it in its own apartment, in a form the system can actually use.

We do not force a template on you. We do not guess. We do not mix your knowledge with your neighbor's. Your business intelligence stays yours.

Three doors in, one apartment

  • 🌐 Website door, give us your website address. We read what you already say to the world, services, hours, policies, your words, and pull out the frameworks: scheduling, someone is coming, return policy, follow-up.
  • 📄 Document door, give us Word, PDF, Excel, how-to documents. We read them, break them into pieces, keep the source and the version on every piece, and put those pieces in your own knowledge base, so an answer can always show where it came from.
  • 💬 Human door, we interview the owner, the checker, and the operator, in plain words. What they agree on becomes verified Q&A, and that becomes the Brief.

How you learn from it, and how we learn from you

After ingestion, we show it back in one Brief: "Here's what we heard your business is, in your words." You read it, you red-pen it. "No, that's wrong, we don't do that, we do this." That correction is kept, with source and version, and it becomes a test the system must pass next time. That is how the system learns from you, not from a generic template. You are the teacher, the system is the student.

We build only what you approve, in the Brief, before anything is built. That is the anti-Salesforce rule: Doma adjusts around the client, the client does not map to Doma.

❓ How we learn to ask, the methodology

Our secret sauce is how we ask. Not the model. Not the voice. The questions.

We ask the right questions, in the right order, and we adjust the next question based on the answer we just heard. Ask once, remember everything, never make you repeat yourself. We learn your questions by asking three people, in plain words:

  • The owner, what should happen, what you promise.
  • The checker, where it breaks, what goes wrong.
  • The operator, what actually happens, on the floor, on the phone, in the truck.

When those three disagree, that disagreement is the most valuable thing we learn. Then we show it all back to you in one brief, your own expertise, reflected back, so there are no surprises. Every interaction after that teaches the system again: what worked, what bobbled, what to ask next time. That is the learning loop, and that is how the questions get smarter without getting pushier.

The agentic blending is the whole thing working as one: your website, your chat, your phone, your text, your live transfer, one conversation, one memory, one record. A visitor can start on the website, move to a call, and end with a person, without repeating anything.
